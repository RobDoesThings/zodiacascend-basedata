### Folklore behind each spirit

- **Gatecarp** — The carp that leaps the falls at the Dragon Gate on the Yellow River and becomes a dragon. *(China)*
- **Needlefin Shoal** — The Malay Annals tell how swordfish (todak) attacked Singapura, until a boy suggested a wall of banana trunks to catch their beaks. *(Malay world (Singapore))*
- **Thornpuff** — The pufferfish, a delicacy so dangerous that only licensed cooks may prepare it. *(East Asia)*
- **Moonshell Snail** — The belief that a turban snail that lives thirty years in the sea becomes a spirit (sazae-oni). *(Japan)*
- **Rain Snail** — The Snail Maiden (Nàng Ốc; 田螺姑娘), a snail kept kindly in a jar who secretly comes out to cook and clean for the lonely person who saved it. *(Vietnam and China)*
- **Crimson Tidemother** — The great red octopus of Uchiura Bay, whose colour is said to stain the sea and sky. *(Hokkaido (Ainu))*
- **Hooktail Shark** — The isonade of the western coasts, a huge sea creature that hooks sailors from their boats with its barbed tail fin. *(Japan)*
- **Harbour Grandfather** — Cá Ông, the whale venerated by coastal fishing communities as a rescuer of those lost at sea; whales that wash ashore are given funerals and enshrined in whale temples. *(Vietnam)*
- **Wingwhale** — The Zhuangzi's Kun, a fish of the northern dark sea thousands of li long, which transforms into Peng, a bird whose wings are like clouds across the sky. *(China)*
- **Forktail Elder** — The nekomata, a cat that grows a forked tail in great old age and gains strange powers, including making things dance. *(Japan)*
- **Beckoning Cat** — The maneki-neko, the beckoning cat said to have waved a lord into Gōtoku-ji temple moments before lightning struck where he had been standing. *(Japan)*
- **Silverpoint Cat** — The Cat-Book Poems (Tamra Maew), which describe lucky cats such as the silver-blue Si Sawat, and the folk rain ritual (Hae Nang Maew) in which a cat is carried through the village in a basket and splashed with water to call the rain. *(Thailand)*
- **Shrine Dove** — Doves as messengers of Hachiman, the god of archery and protection; the first character of Hachiman's name is often drawn as a pair of doves. *(Japan)*
- **Pebble Finch** — Jingwei, a princess who drowned in the Eastern Sea and became a bird that carries pebbles and twigs to fill the sea, forever. *(China)*
- **Three-Step Sunrook** — The three-legged crow that lives in the sun (sanzuwu; samjok-o; yatagarasu), and the legend of the archer who shot down nine of ten suns. *(China, Korea and Japan)*
- **Gulfmaw** — The Bakunawa, a sea serpent that rises to swallow the moon, and the custom of beating pots and drums to make it spit the moon back out. *(Philippines (Visayas))*
- **Dreamgrazer** — The baku, a chimera that devours nightmares; people once kept its picture under their pillows. *(Japan (from China))*
- **Goldmaw** — The pixiu, a winged lion that eats gold and silver and never lets any out, kept as a charm to attract and hold wealth. *(China)*
- **Truthmane** — The haetae (haechi), a lion-like beast that tells right from wrong and butts the guilty; its statues guard Gyeongbokgung palace, where they were believed to protect the palace from fire. *(Korea)*
- **Riverhop Mousedeer** — Sang Kancil, the trickster mousedeer, who crosses a river by persuading the crocodiles to line up so the King can "count" them. *(Malay world (Malaysia, Indonesia, Brunei))*
- **Lampsquid** — The firefly squid of Toyama Bay, whose spring spawning lights the shoreline blue. *(Japan)*
- **Blinkswarm** — The synchronous fireflies of the Selangor River mangroves, which flash in unison on the riverbank trees. *(Malaysia)*
- **Mossback Tortoise** — The minogame, a tortoise so old that seaweed grows from its shell like a long tail; a symbol of long life. *(Japan)*
- **Ironeater** — The bulgasari, a creature shaped from grains of rice that eats iron and grows larger with every meal. *(Korea)*
- **Jewelwing Beetle** — The Tamamushi Shrine at Hōryū-ji, a seventh-century cabinet once decorated with thousands of iridescent jewel-beetle wings. *(Japan)*

## Crafting

**Check:** d20 + discipline ability modifier + Rank (if proficient in the discipline) vs recipe DC.

- **ruined** (Fail by 5 or more): All materials are lost.
- **failed** (Fail by 1–4): No progress; half the materials (round down) are lost.
- **standard** (Meet the DC): The piece is made.
- **fine** (Beat the DC by 5 or more): +2 durability, and pick one: −1 weight class step for Speed purposes, or +1 to the material's special effect.
- **masterwork** (Beat the DC by 10 or more, or a natural 20 that also meets the DC): Fine, plus an inscription slot for a talisman charm (see charms).

**Element synergy:** materials that generate each other −2 DC; materials that control each other +3 DC. The best material's tier sets a minimum DC (T1 10, T2 13, T3 16, T4 19).

## Sample lore

**Nezrath, The First Hour.** When the world was a closed egg of darkness, the story goes, a rat gnawed a hole in it at midnight, and the first breath of air came through. Nezrath claims to be that rat, or its heir. It holds court in the hour between days, where nothing is quite yesterday or tomorrow, and every one of its echoes swears to be the real one.

**Gyuurok, The Unbroken Yoke.** Gyuurok carried the Rat across the river in the Great Race and finished second without a word of complaint. When asked why it never resented the trick, it said it had agreed to carry the Rat, and a promise kept is never a loss. Its yoke is not iron but a vow, and no one has ever seen it break one. Farmers say a family with a buffalo has a future, and the whole Hour of the Buffalo is Gyuurok's family.

**Meonguyet, The Jade Moon.** They say the Rabbit was meant to run the Great Race but would not leave its mortar on the moon, where it pounds medicine still. A white cat ran in its place, and the Hour has been the Cat's ever since. Meonguyet keeps the Rabbit's work on earth: it carries a jade mortar, pounds medicine every night and gives it freely. On clear nights it looks up and nods to the shape in the moon.
